# Deck 1: Mesopotamia and the Call

## Where Avraham started

| Term | Definition | Hook |
|---|---|---|
| Mesopotamia | The land between the Tigris and Euphrates rivers, part of the Fertile Crescent, where Avraham was born. | Between the rivers. |
| Ur | An important city in southern Mesopotamia with irrigation canals, temples, writing and long-distance trade. | Avraham's starting point. |
| Canaan | Region on the eastern Mediterranean with hill country and valleys, farmed with rain rather than river irrigation, and already inhabited. | The land G-d will show. |
| Haran | City in northern Mesopotamia on the route between Ur and Canaan, where Avraham was living when G-d called him. | The stop before the call. |
| Dangers of travel | Journeys took weeks or months and risked weather, hard terrain, lack of water, illness, theft and attack. | The cost of the road. |

## The call (Genesis 12)

| Term | Definition | Hook |
|---|---|---|
| Lech lecha | G-d's command in Genesis 12:1 to go forth from your land, your birthplace and your father's house to the land I will show you. | Go — to an unknown place. |
| Three things left behind | Genesis 12:1 lists land, birthplace and father's house instead of simply saying 'leave.' | Land, birthplace, father's house. |
| Avraham's silence | Genesis 12:4 says only that Abram went as G-d had commanded; he asks no questions. | He just went. |
| Suggests vs. proves | Avraham's quick departure points toward trust or obedience, but the text does not establish his inner reasons. | Evidence vs. inference. |

## Why Avraham?

| Term | Definition | Hook |
|---|---|---|
| Torah's silence on the choice | Genesis 12 gives no reason why G-d chose Avraham; G-d simply speaks to him. | No reason given. |
| Bereishit Rabbah 39:1 | A midrash compiled around the 5th century CE: a traveler sees a lit palace and asks if it has no owner, and the owner looks out. | The palace parable. |
| Rambam on Avraham | Maimonides writes in the Mishneh Torah that, as a child with no teacher among idolaters in Ur, the future patriarch reasoned his way to one G-d. | He figured it out alone. |
| Who took the first step? | Class debate: G-d chose Avraham (Genesis 12), Avraham chose G-d (Rambam), or they chose each other (the midrash). | Three positions. |
| Belief alone isn't enough | Believing in one G-d does not by itself make someone Jewish; what begins in Genesis 12 is a covenant with obligations. | Belief plus commitment. |
| Why begin with Avraham | Your notes suggest the story starts with Avraham because the covenant and its sign, brit milah, begin with him, making him the first Jew. | First covenant, first Jew. |

# Deck 2: Avraham's Character

| Avraham and Lot | Genesis 13:5–12: when their herdsmen quarrel, Avraham lets Lot choose his land first to keep the peace. | You pick first. |

# Deck 3: The Covenant

## What a covenant is

| Term | Definition | Hook |
|---|---|---|
| Brit | The Hebrew word for covenant: a relationship in which both sides make commitments and both receive something. | Two-way bond. |
| Covenant vs. command | A command obligates only the one who obeys; a covenant obligates both sides. | Order vs. bond. |
| Covenant vs. simple deal | A covenant is an ongoing relationship passed down through generations, not a one-time exchange. | A bond, not a receipt. |

## Promises and obligations

| Term | Definition | Hook |
|---|---|---|
| Avraham's obligation | Avraham must leave his land, birthplace and father's house and trust G-d enough to go to an unknown land. | Leave and trust. |
| Great nation | G-d's first promise in Genesis 12:2: 'I will make of you a great nation.' | Many descendants. |
| Make your name great | G-d's promise in Genesis 12:2 that Avraham will be famous and remembered. | Remembered. |
| You shall be a blessing | G-d's promise that Avraham, and those who come from him, will be a source of blessing to others. | Blessing outward. |
| All the families of the earth | Genesis 12:3: all the families of the earth shall bless themselves by Avraham. | Reaching everyone. |
| Bless those who bless you | Genesis 12:3: 'I will bless those who bless you and curse the one who curses you.' | Protection promise. |
| Uneven trade | Avraham's side is one costly act now; G-d's side is several promises that unfold over generations. | Now vs. later. |

## How the covenant develops

| Term | Definition | Hook |
|---|---|---|
| Brit milah | Circumcision, the physical sign of the covenant; your notes suggest it makes Avraham the first Jew. | The covenant in the body. |
| Covenant of the pieces | Genesis 15, where G-d tells Avraham his descendants will be strangers and slaves in a foreign land before returning (about 400 years). | Slavery foretold. |
| Genesis 15:16 | "The fourth generation shall return here, for the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet complete." | Not yet. |
| Righteousness and justice | Your notes: G-d chose Avraham to uphold righteousness and justice in his house, a duty passed to his descendants. | Pass it on. |
| Covenant at Sinai | At Mount Sinai the covenant is offered to the whole people, with conditions: 'if you obey My voice and keep My covenant.' | From one man to a nation. |
| Kingdom of priests and a holy nation | Exodus 19:6: what G-d says Israel will be if they keep the covenant. | A holy nation. |
| Naaseh v'Nishmah | The people's answer at Sinai, 'we will do and we will obey,' given with one voice. | Do first. |
| Deuteronomy 27:9–10 | Forty years later, Moshe tells the new generation, 'This day you have become the people of the Lord.' | Renewed, not inherited. |

## Responsibility

| Term | Definition | Hook |
|---|---|---|
| Guilt vs. responsibility | Yom Kippur lesson: you may not have caused a problem and still have responsibility for what happens next. | Not my fault, still my job. |
| Teshuvah | Return and repair: 'I will sometimes fall short. I can return, repair, and act.' | Return, repair, act. |
| Ways to step up | Interrupt, support, address, involve, change: more than one way to act when something is wrong. | Five ways to act. |

# Deck 7: Israel: Mandate and Government

## From the Ottomans to partition

| Term | Definition | Hook |
|---|---|---|
| Ottoman rule | Your notes: the Turkish empire based in Istanbul controlled the land of Israel and present-day Palestine from 1517 to 1917. | Four centuries of Ottomans. |
| Mandate system | After World War I, the League of Nations had an established nation govern a territory and help its people prepare to rule themselves. | Help them set up. |

## How the government works

| Term | Definition | Hook |
|---|---|---|
| Knesset | Israel's parliament, with 120 members. | 120 seats. |
| Parliamentary democracy | Israel's system: voters elect a parliament, and the government needs the support of a Knesset majority. | Govern by majority. |
| Voting for a party | Israelis vote for a party list, not directly for a prime minister or individual members. | Party, not person. |
| Israel's president | A mainly ceremonial head of state who appoints the person who will try to form a government. | Ceremonial, but picks who tries. |
| Electoral threshold | A party needs about 3.25% of the national vote to enter the Knesset. | Clear the bar. |
| Coalition | Parties that agree to work together to reach at least 61 seats and form a government. | Many parties, one government. |
| Opposition | Parties that are not part of the governing coalition. | Not in government. |
| Largest party may not govern | The party with the most seats governs only if it can build a 61-seat coalition; otherwise another leader may try. | Most ≠ majority. |
| Bloc | A group of parties that usually work together, such as the Netanyahu bloc. | Allied parties. |
| Pro of coalitions | Smaller and minority parties gain influence because larger parties need their seats. | Small parties matter. |
| Con of coalitions | Coalitions can be fragile: if partners withdraw, the government can fall and new elections may follow. | Fragile. |
| Ruled-out partners | Party leaders often promise not to sit with certain rivals, which makes reaching 61 harder. | Red lines. |
