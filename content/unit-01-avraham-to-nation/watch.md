# Watch out

Notes where your sources disagree, are easy to misread, or need checking. Each starts with the deck it belongs to.

- (deck 1) Same people, different spellings: Avram/Abram = Avraham/Abraham; Sarai = Sarah; Yitzchak = Isaac; Yaakov = Jacob = Israel; Yishmael = Ishmael. Your packets switch between them, and so may the test.
- (deck 1) Genesis 12 gives no reason for choosing Avraham. The idea that he discovered G-d as a child comes from Rambam; the palace story comes from the midrash (Bereishit Rabbah 39:1). If a question asks what "the Torah says," don't pick the midrash or Rambam.
- (deck 2) Evidence vs. inference: Genesis 12:4 says only that Avram went. "He was brave," "he was a sheep" and "he trusted G-d" are inferences. Your study guide says the response "suggests (not proves)."
- (deck 3) Your packets don't print Genesis 15 or 17. Brit milah as the covenant's sign and "maybe Avraham is the first Jew" come from your class notes. Genesis 15:16 (the fourth generation, the Amorites) is printed in Lesson 2.2.
- (deck 4) On the family-tree chart, Issachar and Zebulun are drawn in the box under Bilhah, but they are Leah's sons (her 9th and 10th children on the chart's numbering). Bilhah's sons are Dan and Naphtali. VERIFY with your teacher if a question depends on it.
- (deck 4) Dinah is Yaakov's daughter. The 12 tribes come from his 12 sons; she isn't one of them.
- (deck 5) Family / people / nation: Genesis 46 (70 persons) = family. Exodus 1 ("the Israelite people") = people. A nation also has land, laws and government; Sinai and the Mishkan add laws and worship, and entering the land adds a homeland.
- (deck 5) Lesson 2.2's chart: "Torah says" (Genesis 15:16) is different from "we can observe" and "historians may infer." Don't treat an inference as a quote.
- (deck 6) Your notes don't give a formal definition of antisemitism. The kit uses your study guide's wording: hostility toward Jews as Jews. If your teacher gave a specific definition in class, use that (VERIFY).
- (deck 6) Criticizing a government or policy is not the same as hostility toward Jews as Jews. Your note: supporting Palestinians doesn't by itself mean hostility to Jews.
- (deck 7) The 3.25% threshold is the minimum to ENTER the Knesset. A party that passes it gets about 4 seats, not 1. Your Israeli Gov notes say "to get 1 seat," which is not quite right.
- (deck 7) Israel's president is mainly ceremonial but DOES appoint the person who tries to form a government. You missed this on the quick check.
- (deck 7) The election numbers in your JTA assignment are snapshots (poll, exit poll, partial count). Don't treat a poll as a result.
- (deck 7) Partition: your notes say the split was unequal, a bad deal for Jews that they accepted, while the Arabs got the better part and rejected it. That is your class's summary; the packets don't give the details or the year.
- (deck 2) Dates in your notes (Avraham about 2000 BCE, Islam "638 CE," Christianity "300 CE") look uncertain. Don't rely on exact dates; questions are about ideas and evidence.
